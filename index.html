Academic Article Summary

Osatuyi and Passerini (2016) examine how social media technologies influence student engagement and academic performance. Their study compares Twitter-based course discussions with a traditional online discussion board over approximately fourteen weeks. The Twitter group achieved higher average final grades (about 94%) than the forum group (about 79%). Students who participated more actively tended to perform better, and network analysis showed that students’ positions in online conversations were linked to achievement. Participants described Twitter as flexible and enjoyable, while the forum felt more formal. The authors suggest social media can support collaboration, while noting the study does not establish direct causation.

Reference: Osatuyi, B., & Passerini, K. (2016). Twittermania: Understanding how social media technologies impact engagement and academic performance of a new generation of learners. Communications of the Association for Information Systems, 39, 509–528. https://doi.org/10.17705/1CAIS.03923

Reflecting on My Last Teaching Period

University study is not only about earning a qualification. It also shows you how well you can handle new challenges. In the last teaching period, I studied New Media and Digital Communication at Swinburne University. This essay reflects on what I studied, the doubts I faced, how I feel about my results, and what I plan to do differently next time.

During the teaching period, I focused on how digital technology changes the way people communicate. My main assessment was a case study on the influence of artificial intelligence on Gen Z's academic performance and communication skills. I had to find academic sources, apply ideas such as digitisation and personalisation, and write a focused 800-word report. I found the topic interesting because it relates to my own life. I use AI tools and social media every day, so the research made me think more carefully about my own habits.

However, the teaching period was not easy, and I had real doubts. The first was the academic assignments. Writing a case study with APA referencing, a strict word limit and a clear argument was very different from what I was used to, and at times I wondered whether I could meet the standard. The second was language. I was changing from studying in Arabic to studying in English, which meant understanding lectures, reading academic articles and expressing complex ideas in a language that is not my first. Sometimes I knew what I wanted to say but could not find the right words quickly. These moments made me question whether this course was the right choice for me.

Even so, I am happy with my results, and they gave me confidence. They showed me that my doubts were about the challenge, not about my ability. Every assignment I completed improved my English, and my research and writing skills became stronger. I now believe this is the right field for me, because it connects theory to the real world and leads to careers such as social media, content strategy and corporate communication.

Based on this experience, I plan to do some things differently next teaching period. First, I will start assignments earlier, so I have time to edit and check my English. Second, I will use university support, such as tutors and writing and language help, before I submit work, not after. Finally, I will read English articles and practise academic vocabulary every week, so writing feels more natural.

In conclusion, the last teaching period was challenging but worthwhile. The academic demands and the change from Arabic to English made me doubt myself, but my good results proved that I can succeed. With better planning and more support, I expect to do even better next time.
